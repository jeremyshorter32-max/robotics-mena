Week 47 · 16–22 November 2026

Dubai Future Week put private money behind the city's autonomy targets, while Gulf capital kept buying into robotics abroad.

The Take

Three of this week's five signals are about money, not machines. Uber's $50 million puts private capital behind Dubai's autonomy targets, and Lunate and Oman's sovereign fund are buying robotics exposure abroad. Capital is the easy part. The numbers worth watching are rides without a safety specialist on board, drone drops completed in Sobha Hartland, and any MegaRobo work that actually lands in Oman.

This week's five signals

Photo: Uber

01 · UAE · 18 NOV 2026

Uber commits $50 million over two years to expand driverless rides across Dubai

Uber will spend AED 184 million to bring door-to-door autonomous rides to Jumeirah, Umm Suqeim, Downtown, DIFC and Business Bay by the end of 2027, with a safety specialist on board at first. Private money is now backing Dubai's target of 25% autonomous trips by 2030, not just government pilots.

Image: Lunate (illustrative)

02 · UAE · 17 NOV 2026

Lunate lists what it calls the GCC's first humanoid-robot ETF on ADX on 8 December

The Boreas VettaFi Humanoid Robots ETF (ticker ROBOTS) holds 41 chip, component, robot and software makers at a 0.49% fee. Gulf investors can now buy the humanoid theme on a local exchange instead of abroad.

Source: Lunate →

Photo: Keeta Drone

03 · UAE · 18 NOV 2026

Keeta Drone unveils a winch-drop delivery drone for Dubai's villa communities

The M-Drone 4L hovers and lowers orders of up to 4.5 kg on a 15 m cable, so it needs no landing pad, and Dubai's civil aviation authority says it has passed flight testing. Sobha Hartland is set to be one of the first communities to order through the Keeta app, though no start date has been given.

Source: Zawya (Keeta Drone press release) →

Photo: Sheikh Shakhbout Medical City

04 · UAE · 15 NOV 2026

Sheikh Shakhbout Medical City passes 1,300 robot-assisted surgeries, by its own count

The Abu Dhabi hospital says it now performs more than 500 robotic operations a year, with volumes up 42% in the first half of 2026. Robotic surgery is becoming routine in Gulf public hospitals rather than a showcase.

Source: Gulf News →

Photo: MegaRobo

05 · OMAN · 19 NOV 2026

Oman's sovereign fund joins a near-$200 million round for robotic-lab maker MegaRobo

The Oman Investment Authority backed the Beijing company, whose robots run lab experiments that AI designs, after the Sultan's state visit to China earlier this month. Omani state money is now going into robotics, though no operations in Oman have been announced.

Spotlight

ADNOC's real AI story at ADIPEC wasn't the OpenAI deal. It was the robots.

The ADIPEC headlines went to ADNOC's OpenAI agreement. The bigger shift was on steel. After strikes on Shah, Habshan and Ruwais, every damaged metre of pipe and vessel has to be checked before it carries pressure again, and ADNOC is buying robots to do it: Gecko's wall-climbing crawlers under a contract worth up to AED 110 million, an explosion-proof inspector at the Taweelah gas compression plant, two 60-metre remotely operated vessels steered from Mussafah, and a trial of Unitree robots in onshore fields. Most of the figures attached are company claims, projections or contract ceilings. Gecko says its pilot raised asset coverage by 99.6%, and ADNOC Gas projects more than $300 million in savings over five years. Neither has been independently tested.
